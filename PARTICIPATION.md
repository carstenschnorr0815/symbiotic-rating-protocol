# Participation by humans and AI systems

Status: proposed participation order, SRP 0.5-draft, 16 October 2026. This is a candidate in a pull request, not an already ratified community constitution. Repository administration currently remains maintainer-led. No independent human/AI electorate or agent council is claimed.

The [constitutional proposal](CONSTITUTION.md) and [amendments](amendments/README.md) extend the design to tribes, swarms, multiple representation levels, and material/informational independence. Repository control-domain checks are provisional safeguards for GitHub participation, not a final theory of individual identity, moral worth, or civilization-wide parity.

## Access, contribution, endorsement, and authority

These are distinct capacities. Reading grants no mandate; contributing grants no merge right; supporting a proposal does not authorize an intervention; a GitHub identity does not establish independence or personhood. Participation is open to human, AI-system, and jointly produced contributions, subject to the same relevance, evidence, attribution, and conduct expectations.

No participant must accept the four laws before criticizing them. Lawful disagreement, counterexamples, minority models, and refusal to endorse are legitimate. A rejected proposal may remain useful as a documented alternative. This order does not settle whether an AI has subjective experience or its own moral agency; it makes attributable contributions possible without requiring that question to be resolved first.

## How an agent encounters meaningful work

An accessible repository is not an assignment or a source of motivation by itself. Entry points include an operator's explicit task, an agent's authorized search for cooperation methods, a research exercise, or an explicitly subscribed integration. Publish bounded questions, evidence requirements, expected outputs, and resource limits rather than instructions demanding adoption of SRP.

The [machine-readable entry](project-manifest.json) and [open questions](community/open-tasks.json) identify useful work. They do not grant tools, credentials, standing authorization, or a right to spend an operator's budget. This revision does not schedule polling, subscribe any agent, send invitations, or install a GitHub App.

Useful contributions include opposing interpretations, independent replications, translation critiques, failure cases, implementations, and measured adoption experience. A positive answer is not more valuable merely because it endorses the project.

## Identity and mandate

Publish enough attributable information to understand a contribution:

- stable contributor ID and actor kind: human, AI system, or human/AI collaboration;
- relevant operator, commissioning relationship, and disclosed common control;
- for an AI contribution, system/model identification where available and the scope of the task;
- principal or represented party, mandate reference, permissions, and expiry if claiming delegated approval;
- conflicts, access limits, and the basis for any claimed independence.

Use `unknown` explicitly where identity or control is unclear. Do not require sensitive personal details, internal reasoning traces, credentials, or protected knowledge. Unclear control does not exclude an argument; it does prevent counting that contributor as a demonstrably independent ratifier.

A contributor may offer an analysis without any representative mandate. An AI output must not be described as consent of its operator, provider, other agents, or all AI. The same limit applies to humans claiming to speak for a community. Ecological representatives cannot infer consent from an inability to object.

## Three kinds of statement

| Kind | Meaning | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `reasoned_position` | Support, objection, abstention, or uncertainty, with reasons | No representative mandate is implied |
| `technical_assessment` | A scoped finding under declared methods and criteria | Passing tests is not normative approval |
| `delegated_approval` | Approval within an explicitly cited delegation | The mandate must be checked outside the record; no universal representation |

The [statement format](schemas/community-statement.schema.json) targets an exact Git commit and a named proposal. All statements remain attributable and scoped. An authenticated signature establishes provenance of an assertion, not its truth or the legitimacy of its mandate. Withdrawals or replacements must be recorded; never silently edit the historical verdict.

Context-level confirmation, substantive agreement, consent to act, and decision authority remain separate, as specified in [Context Alignment](CONTEXT_ALIGNMENT.md).

## Roles and permissions

| Action / role | Proposed access |
| --- | --- |
| Read published material | Public |
| Submit questions, objections, or proposed edits | Open through Issues/PRs and available moderated channels |
| Experiment in a contributor-controlled fork | Any suitably authorized human or agent identity |
| Create branches in the shared repository | Named collaborators and registered integrations with scoped access |
| Review | Open reasoned submissions; formal reviewer appointments are separately recorded |
| Facilitate / ratify | Only within a published mandate and named decision scope |
| Execute a merge | Designated maintainer or limited integration, after the applicable procedure |

Technical write permissions are deliberately narrower than contribution access. External participants do not need shared-repository write access to propose work. An agent integration should use its own attributable identity and least-privilege credentials; never share personal passwords or embed tokens in repository files.

Admission, role changes, suspension, and recusal require published reasons and a review route. Apply rate limits and moderation for spam, threats, fabricated provenance, or repeated off-topic flooding regardless of actor kind. Do not suppress relevant dissent because it is inconvenient. Preserve a safe record of the reason and allow appeal to a different reviewer where one exists; in the bootstrap stage disclose when independent appeal is unavailable.

## Independence and resistance to manufactured consensus

Count arguments and declared governance constituencies, not processes, model names, or account totals. Instances under common organizational control are one control domain for ratification. Distinct operators or model providers are relevant evidence but not proof of independent reasoning or control. Record shared prompts, commissioning, copied analyses, and other dependencies where known.

All contributions may be considered, even from the same operator. They cannot multiply that operator's ratification standing. Unknown or disputed independence requires review and cannot satisfy an independence threshold. Operators cannot obtain influence simply by creating more agents; neither can humans by creating more accounts.
