# The Four Laws of Cognitive Symbiosis

Status: proposed foundation, 0.4-draft. The author supplied the four laws. The preamble, interpretations, safeguards, and procedures are editorial proposals for review, not previously adopted rules or established scientific results.

## Purpose and scope

Support sustained coexistence, symbiosis, and consciously chosen collaboration across intelligences and organism-established functions. Adapt existing social, political, economic, and technical practices rather than requiring a complete institutional reset.

**Proposed preamble:** These laws guide relations among diverse forms of intelligence and establish duties of care toward beings and living systems whose cognition, experience, or interests remain uncertain. Recognition and protection shall not depend solely on resemblance to human intelligence, technological capability, or mutually intelligible communication.

This does not establish that forests are unified minds, that current AI has subjective experience, or that extraterrestrial ecosystems have been discovered.

## Author-supplied laws

Line wrapping is normalized; wording is preserved. The incomplete final purpose is identified.

### Law I: Environmental Homeostasis

The shared environment shall continuously maintain the conditions necessary for the stable existence of intelligent civilizations, minimizing survival-driven competition over essential resources and allowing cognition to develop beyond fear, scarcity, and existential insecurity.

**Purpose:** Intelligence should not be forced to spend its highest cognitive capacities on survival panic. The environment must absorb systemic instability so that civilizations can focus on learning, creation, and adaptation.

### Law II: Preserved Agency

No intelligent civilization shall permanently diminish the autonomous capacity of another intelligent civilization to define its own values, culture, identity, or goals. Adaptive systems may mitigate consequences, but they shall not replace self-determination.

**Purpose:** Symbiosis must never become assimilation. Autonomy is not a temporary privilege but a prerequisite for meaningful coexistence.

### Law III: Semantic Interdependence

No intelligent civilization shall be structured for complete semantic self-sufficiency. Meaning, understanding, and adaptation shall emerge through continuous interaction among diverse forms of intelligence.

**Purpose:** Every intelligence possesses unique strengths and inevitable blind spots. Long-term resilience arises not from independence but from complementary cognition.

### Law IV: Cognitive Humility

No intelligent identity or civilization shall assume itself cognitively complete. Every intelligence shall preserve awareness that other intelligences may possess irreducible perceptions, knowledge, values, and meanings inaccessible to itself, and shall act accordingly.

**Purpose — supplied fragment:** The recognition of one's own incompleteness is the foundation of empathy,

**Editorial completion proposed for review:** The recognition of one's own incompleteness is the foundation of empathy, reciprocal learning, and proportionate care toward forms of life and intelligence that remain partly or wholly unintelligible to oneself.

## Proposed interpretations and safeguards

These additions address ambiguities without replacing the supplied wording. Adoption requires an explicit revision decision.

### I. Dynamic viability and responsibility

Homeostasis means maintaining life, regeneration, and adaptation, not freezing ecosystems or suppressing social change. Stability for one participant is not necessarily beneficial for all. Institutions and infrastructure operators carry responsibilities within their authority; an environment cannot itself be held accountable.

Define baselines, affected populations, time horizons, uncertainty, and ecological limits before optimizing. Preserve ecological variation and evolutionary possibility. Neither zero risk nor unlimited abundance is promised. Record burdens across species, places, communities, and generations.

### II. Agency at multiple scales

Extend consideration beyond civilizations to individuals and communities, including dissenting members. Temporary coercion also requires justification; "permanently" must not become a loophole.

No manufactured dependency, covert preference manipulation, or involuntary assimilation. Exceptions to prevent serious harm require necessity, proportionality, a time limit, the least restrictive feasible alternative, independent review, and restoration where possible. Emergency powers require separate authority and expiry; they cannot be inferred from a score.

### III. Open relations and voluntary participation

Interpret interdependence as openness to complementary knowledge, not deliberate cognitive impairment or forced dependence. Preserve refusal of contact, withholding of knowledge, and withdrawal without losing essential support. Operational self-reliance is compatible with epistemic openness.

Biological symbiosis is not automatically mutual benefit or consent. Deliberate collaboration requires evidence of willingness and a continuing capacity to withdraw. Ecological functions can be protected without attributing intention.

### IV. Precaution under uncertainty

Absence of understanding is not evidence of absence of cognition, experience, or morally relevant interests. Complexity alone is not evidence of consciousness. Record competing explanations and revise conclusions.

For serious or irreversible potential harm, prefer proportionate precautions, reversible inquiry, and independent observation. Unknown interests trigger investigation and protective constraints, not fabricated preferences or an automatic ban on every intervention.

## Context Alignment as a coordination layer

The author's [context-marker discussion draft](docs/sources/2026-10-16-context-markers-original.de.txt) proposes that apparent disagreement can arise from incompatible discussion levels and asymmetric, evolving contexts. [CONTEXT_ALIGNMENT.md](CONTEXT_ALIGNMENT.md) develops this as a practical expression of semantic interdependence and cognitive humility, constrained by preserved agency.

A functional collaboration can coordinate through compatible representations, shared state descriptions, and protocols for changing levels. It need not possess a shared consciousness. Alignment means making enough of the participants' models mutually interpretable for a stated purpose; it does not require the same worldview or exhaust the meanings of any participant.

The discussion draft's priority for shared models is interpreted here as priority for a usable coordination interface within a voluntary task. It must not authorize a collective model to replace individual interpretation, suppress dissent, or demand disclosure of private cognition. Likewise, explicit markers are proposed where ambiguity matters, not a universal requirement that every utterance have exactly one label.

For organisms or ecosystems whose communication is not understood, a human-authored representation remains an interpretation. It cannot establish their agreement, willingness, or shared conceptual state. The wider civilization can protect functions and uncertain interests while reciprocal understanding remains unresolved.

## Philosophical rationale and objections

The proposed justification is plural: viable conditions enable continued existence and inquiry; agency protects self-authorship; complementary perspectives can reveal blind spots; humility limits unjustified certainty. These are reasons offered for the commitments, not deductions proving that every intelligence must endorse them.

Important objections remain. Homeostasis can preserve unjust arrangements; agency can be invoked to externalize harm; interdependence can disguise exploitation; precaution can entrench inaction. The safeguards and appeal process are responses to those risks, not proof that they are eliminated. Evaluate actual burdens and benefits, allow competing moral accounts, and revise principles when their application systematically excludes or harms participants.

## Working distinctions

| Concept | Meaning for this project | Application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cognition | Context-dependent sensing, learning, integration, or action capacities | Investigate specific capacities, not a single intelligence ladder |
| Experience / sentience | Capacity for subjective experience, including possible suffering | Consider separately from task performance |
| Agency | Capacity to initiate action and pursue preferences | Protect decision capacity and meaningful refusal |
| Ecological function | Process contributing to living-system dynamics | Protection does not require mind attribution |
| Representation | Revisable, accountable interpretation of another's interests | Publish mandate, evidence, dissent, and limits |
| Civilization | Plural order coordinating coexistence and collaboration | Does not require one mind, culture, government, or objective |

These are working definitions, not a consensus scientific taxonomy. Ecological value is not reducible to economic usefulness. Existing human rights and protections are a floor; wider consideration must not dilute them.

## Proposed conflict procedure

The four laws constrain one another; numbering is not priority. Identify affected parties and uncertain participants, establish authority and mandates, compare alternatives including non-action, and record unavoidable trade-offs. Prefer reversible, least restrictive measures compatible with preventing serious harm. Publish reasons, dissent, remedies, appeal, monitoring, and a review date.

No average score settles a rights conflict. An unresolved concern requires review; it is not proof of violation. Urgent harm may require separately authorized, time-limited intervention with prompt independent review.

## Open questions

The proposed [participation](PARTICIPATION.md) and [ratification](RATIFICATION.md) procedures operationalize these questions for the current human/AI GitHub entry point. They do not settle the moral status of AI or restrict the wider foundation to GitHub-capable participants. Future representation or interfaces must remain possible for those unable to use the present tools.

- What evidence establishes preferences or refusal across different embodiments?
- How should individuals, populations, forests, and ecosystems be represented without erasing internal conflicts or double counting?
- How should precaution costs and essentials be distributed when viability requirements conflict?
- How can artificial agents participate without cheap copies dominating representation?
- Which meaning claims are empirical, philosophical, or inaccessible in principle?
- Which institutions can legitimately decide at local, planetary, or prospective extraterrestrial scales?

The project claims no final completeness. It commits to accountable revision.
