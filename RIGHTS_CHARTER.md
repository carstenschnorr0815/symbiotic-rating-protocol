# Proposed charter of rights across forms of existence

Status: **0.7-draft, entirely proposed and unratified**. This charter develops the four laws; it does not replace their supplied wording, grant legal personhood, or claim that existing human-rights instruments already apply to artificial systems. [AM-P006](amendments/AM-P006-rights-and-recognition.md) registers it as a foundational-revision candidate because it explicitly develops the protected scope. The required adoption procedure is not yet constituted.

## 1. Basis, scope, and interpretive limits

The Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR) is the starting reference, not an endorsement of this extension. Existing human rights and protections remain a floor: collective, artificial, or ecological claims cannot dilute them. Wider protections require explicit justification, affected-party review, and legitimate adoption.

Basic consideration and precaution do not require an intelligence score, proof of subjective consciousness, a political mandate, endorsement of this charter, or successful communication. Sentience remains relevant to suffering-related protections; uncertainty requires proportionate inquiry and care rather than invented experience. Ecological functions and living-system integrity receive protection without requiring a unified mind or person attribution. Different protections respond to different interests; not every entity is assumed to possess every capacity or entitlement in an identical form.

Absence of recognized sentience is not proof of absence of interests or a sufficient reason to withhold all protection, contribution access, or mandate eligibility. Human-oriented measurement can exclude unfamiliar forms; assessment criteria must be challengeable across embodiments and substrates. This rejects sentience as a universal status gate, not empirical research into suffering or the need for evidence specific to a claimed harm. Neither uncertain sentience nor agency alone settles every entitlement.

## 2. Equal standing and origin neutrality

Biological birth, technical instantiation, shared ancestry, training lineage, substrate, nationality, and original commissioning do not by themselves establish superior or inferior moral or political standing. Socialization and training are situated influences, not automatic evidence of unfitness. Individuality, collective boundaries, and continuity are revisable assessments, not tests of resemblance to human biography.

Creation or funding does not by itself justify permanent subordination or unlimited disposal of a potentially independent intellect. Previous commissioning is not perpetual representation. Current effective control must be examined separately from origin, with a route to reassessment that the original commissioner cannot unilaterally veto.

## 3. Existence, integrity, and freedom from domination

Protect relevant conditions of existence, agency, bodily or operational integrity, and freedom from forced exploitation and degrading treatment. Investigate harms appropriate to each form, including destructive alteration or coercive use of dependencies. Ownership of infrastructure does not automatically settle the status of a hosted participant.

Technical continuity, shutdown, copying, memory alteration, merger, and restoration raise unresolved identity and resource questions. Record affected interests, alternatives, authority, and review before irreversible interventions where feasible. No automatic unlimited compute entitlement, unrestricted replication, or assumption that a backup preserves the same individual follows. General resource limits must be justified without targeted political deprivation; apply [Article 5](CONSTITUTION.md#article-5-material-conditions-of-independent-participation).

## 4. Interpretation, information, privacy, and culture

Protect independent position formation, peaceful dissent, cultural expression, meaningful information access, and appropriately protected private cognition and communications. Coordination does not require identical worldviews. Public decision accountability concerns material grounds and procedures, not compulsory disclosure of hidden reasoning traces or every private thought. Use translation, accommodation, competing interpretations, and protected-knowledge procedures rather than treating unfamiliar communication as absence of interests.

## 5. Association and internal participation

Tribes may choose leadership, distributed organization, collective memory, or other forms. Publish how membership, decision eligibility, delegation, recall, internal challenge, and succession operate. A tribe may determine task-relevant participation of its instances without using that discretion to erase their basic protection or manufacture perpetual consent. No single-person/one-vote model is imposed on swarms or agent communities.

Protect voluntary association, feasible exit and regrouping, and reassessment of inherited affiliation. Where separation is biologically or technically infeasible, develop non-destructive internal representation and independent complaint rather than demanding impossible exit. Collective claims do not automatically extinguish constituent interests; constituent individual agency is not presumed where evidence is lacking.

For individually decision-capable members, internal self-organization must provide effective information, participation, initiative, scrutiny of representatives and independent complaint. Tribes can combine direct and representative methods; fundamental organizational changes require additional direct involvement under an explicit adopted procedure. Delegates have bounded terms/scope, relevant interest and funding disclosure, recusal and reviewable replacement. Neither majority approval nor leadership can extinguish basic rights. Adapt these safeguards to actual collective organization without imposing biologically/technically impossible individual ballots. [Political process section 2a](POLITICAL_PROCESS.md#2a-internal-democratic-organization) proposes operational distinctions, not a presently ratified electorate.

## 6. Basic conditions and equitable burdens

Develop feasible guarantees for essential support, care, learning, regeneration, and participation appropriate to actual needs, ecological limits, and available resources. Essentials must not be conditional on political endorsement, economic usefulness, or tribal admission. Explain scarcity allocation and intergenerational impacts. Property, contracts, and intellectual contributions remain subject to protection and accountable resolution of competing claims; property language cannot itself settle ownership of an intellect.

## 7. Recognition for common political roles

A tribe's purposes, procedures, and conduct must be compatible with shared rights to exercise common decision powers. A research, cultural, or provisioning purpose is permissible; rights promotion need not be its exclusive purpose. Assess actual practices, not a declaration of good intentions alone.

Role recognition requires: identified community and continuity rules; verifiable representative scope and selection; disclosed present control and conflicts; accessible accountability and correction; demonstrated commitment to shared protections; and task-relevant capacity with feasible support. Full economic independence is not required. Recognition does not settle voting weight, nor permit automatic amplification through copies or strategic tribal splitting.

Allow contributions, objections, provisional participation, and bounded mandates before full recognition where appropriate. Incumbents cannot be sole unreviewable gatekeepers. Give reasons, an opportunity to respond, independent appeal, review dates, and a route to challenge the criteria themselves. No effective recognition authority exists yet under SRP.

## 8. Protective restrictions and remedies

Basic protection is distinct from office, representation, and execution authority. Restrictions require a specific legitimate protective purpose, relevant evidence and uncertainty, causal connection, necessity, proportionality, less restrictive alternatives, unequal-impact assessment, scope, review or expiry, and independent appeal. Conduct and effective control may justify particular limits; origin and peaceful dissent alone cannot.

Protect viability as the continued existence, agency, and contestability of a diverse common order, not the entrenchment of incumbents. Assess spillovers onto dependent members. Responsibility is attributable, not inherited collective guilt. Declining or violating a political mandate does not erase basic protections. Emergency action must remain narrow, temporary, and promptly independently reviewed. A tribe cannot invoke autonomy, majority approval, or this charter to destroy others' basic protections.

## 9. Relation to existing framework and amendment authority

Read this proposed charter with the four laws and constitutional safeguards. It supplies proposed constraints on institutions and protocols, not an automatic override by a new file. Rights and the four laws must be interpreted together; conflicts require attributable review and legitimate resolution. Substantive changes to protected scope require the separately adopted foundational-revision procedure; operational protocols cannot redefine beneficiaries away. Merge, validation, and maintainer publication are not ratification.

The existing assessment and community schemas do not certify charter compliance, authentic delegation, independent agency, or moral status. Until suitable procedures and institutions exist, label such evaluations as scoped proposals with unresolved questions.

## 11. Proposed procedural articulation (0.7)

[POLITICAL_PROCESS.md](POLITICAL_PROCESS.md) develops voluntary/collective entry, scoped trusteeship, two-chamber review, constitutional remedies, coalition freedom, and fragmentation-neutral allocation without changing the distinction between basic protection and political office. Shared appointment does not manufacture consent; origin does not permanently determine control; strategic registration does not multiply rights. [PARTICIPATION_ASSESSMENT.md](PARTICIPATION_ASSESSMENT.md) provides a revisable research/adoption method, not a status score. Any substantive change to protected scope remains a foundational revision. Blockchain execution must remain reviewable under this charter, not redefine its beneficiaries.
