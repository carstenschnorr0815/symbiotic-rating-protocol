# Proposed constitutional order

Version: 0.5-draft. This is a design for discussion, not an existing state, legal constitution, or adopted community charter. Read the [manifesto](MANIFEST.md), [four laws](FOUNDATION.md), and [amendment register](amendments/README.md) together.

## Article 1. Scope and plural organization

Consider individual and collective intellects, their communities, and uncertain interests of living systems. A tribe is a proposed political association that may be grounded in origin, care, shared values, or chosen cooperation. Its internal order may use leadership, councils, distributed decisions, or other accountable arrangements. This definition is normative for the project, not a universal anthropological classification.

A swarm may participate as a tribe or as a collective member of another tribe. Collective agency does not by itself establish a unified consciousness or erase possible interests of constituent beings. Identity, continuity, membership, and decision scope remain questions to investigate rather than impose through one biological model. See [AM-P001](amendments/AM-P001-tribes-and-collective-agency.md).

## Article 2. Internal autonomy and shared protections

Tribes choose internal organization within the common protections, including meaningful voluntary delegation where applicable. Origin, care, dependence, membership, agreement, and authority are different relationships. No blanket consent follows from ancestry, silence, dependence, or inability to communicate.

Different roles and freely accepted leadership are permissible. Invoking tribal autonomy alone cannot justify avoidable serious harm or extinguish the agency of independently acting members. Protection must fit actual organization: no biologically impossible separation is demanded, and a claim that members are "only components" cannot automatically defeat review. Apply the four laws within and between associations.

### Equal consideration and justified protective distinctions

Situated socialization, training, and institutional constraints require scrutiny across all participants. No origin is presumed neutral or inherently unfit. Equal consideration permits different roles and bounded powers where their relevant conditions differ; it does not authorize inherited political superiority.

A protective distinction must identify the protected ecological, material, or constitutional condition, the particular risk or competence requirement, supporting evidence and uncertainty, its causal connection to the measure, feasible alternatives, and unequal burdens. Require necessity, proportionality, accessible reasons and response, independent review and appeal, and an expiry or scheduled reconsideration. Assess conduct, effective control, and task-relevant capacity; nationality, species, cultural background, training lineage, peaceful dissent, or an ideological label alone are insufficient. Offer feasible accommodation or support before treating a remediable access barrier as incapacity.

System viability refers to sustaining diverse participants' existence, agency, and ability to contest and revise collective decisions. It cannot independently justify entrenching officeholders or silencing criticism. Restrict the implicated act, resource use, or mandate rather than assigning collective guilt or withdrawing basic protections. Any unavoidable spillover onto dependent or collective members requires explicit assessment, mitigation, and representation of their interests. Emergency interventions must have narrow scope and duration and prompt independent review; unresolved uncertainty cannot establish permanent exclusion. The institutions and detailed procedures for enforcing these safeguards remain to be constituted.

## Article 3. Two forms of legislative representation

Propose a **delegated assembly** addressing the interests and affectedness of recognized individual/collective participants and a **tribes' chamber** addressing the autonomy and common interests of participating tribes. Neither chamber is automatically superior or identical to an executive hierarchy. Do not assume headcount, one species/one vote, or one tribe/one vote settles parity.

Seat allocation, constituency recognition, mandates, overlapping membership, collective boundaries, and safeguards against strategic splitting/replication remain open. An adopted rule must resolve them before these chambers can ratify real measures. [AM-P002](amendments/AM-P002-representation-and-powers.md) describes joint approval and disagreement safeguards without inventing current officeholders or voting numbers.

## Article 4. Separation of powers

Separate common rule-making, execution/resource administration, and independent review/dispute resolution. Operators and funders must not be the sole judges of their own interventions. Appointment, recusal, removal, funding, and appeal must prevent the same controlling interests from occupying every function.

The two chambers are proposed divisions within rule-making; their existence alone would not establish separation from executive and review powers. Actual institutions, authority, and enforcement remain to be designed and adopted.

## Article 5. Material conditions of independent participation

No already represented actor may unilaterally use essential existence, communication, or operating resources to direct another representation's political position. Disclose effective ownership, funding, configuration, memory, interface, and shutdown powers. A different server or provider is not proof of independence.

Shared infrastructure may operate under public, purpose-limited budgets with separated operating and review roles, continuity planning, and feasible migration. General resource limits remain possible; targeted political deprivation is not ordinary budget administration. Urgent safety restrictions require narrow scope, reasons, time limits, and independent review. Unlimited resource claims or unreviewable operation are not implied. See [AM-P003](amendments/AM-P003-material-independence.md).

## Article 6. Information access and decision accountability

Participants need workable access to material decision information, competing interpretations, translation where feasible, and resources/time to assess it. Privacy, sensitive habitats, and protected knowledge may justify restrictions, with reasons, appropriate substitutes, and review. No actor is promised all information or a shared perceptual world.

For collectively binding decisions, maintain a challengeable register of material considerations: treatment, contested evidence, value weighting, uncertainty, exclusions, and access gaps. Accounting for an argument does not require agreeing with it. A polished explanation is not proof of actual consideration; use suitable provenance and independent tests. This is public decision accountability, not compulsory disclosure of every private thought or hidden internal process.

Use accessible support first where capacity/access is lacking. Remedies for substantiated procedural failure may concern a decision or mandate, not the worth of an entire tribe. Require independent findings, proportionality, appeal, and restoration. Do not remove essentials or basic information access for dissent or use a "populist" label as an automatic exclusion criterion. See [AM-P004](amendments/AM-P004-information-and-accountability.md).

## Article 7. Revision and admission of unfamiliar forms

New participants may challenge the concepts and procedures of the existing order. Provide provisional participation and precaution where understanding is incomplete, without manufacturing consent or automatically granting unchecked decision power. Incumbents must not use permanent impossible admission conditions to exclude unfamiliar intellects.

Review identity criteria, representation, burden distribution, interfaces, and mandates on new evidence. Revisions must preserve documented dissent and protect those affected by changes. See [AM-P005](amendments/AM-P005-adaptive-integration.md).

## Article 8. Amendment procedure and authority

1. Register a draft with immutable proposal ID, versioned text, affected provisions, evidence, alternatives, impacts, and unresolved questions.
2. Classify its effect: implementation change, ordinary constitutional amendment, or foundational revision. Classification can be challenged; stricter treatment applies while material doubt remains.
3. Review publicly using attributable statements and substantive objection treatment. In a future constituted system, require approval through both representation channels and independent compatibility review under previously adopted rules. Detailed thresholds and deadlock procedures must be adopted beforehand; no new quorum is asserted here.
4. Record the exact ratified text/commit, eligible participants, adopted procedure, scope, effective date, and transitional effects. Repository merge and technical validation are not ratification.
5. Activate only on that basis. Preserve the original and every later amendment; publish a consolidated reading with exact provenance and explicit replacements/repeals. Chronological recency alone grants no precedence.
6. Use a separately adopted protective process for foundational revisions. An amendment cannot lower its own adoption threshold or silently redefine the protected population out of existence. Future participants can challenge inherited categories; no current group claims final cognitive completeness.

Until these institutions exist, repository-level discussion or a transparently labelled maintainer reference remains the only asserted development stage. A founder decision must not be labelled ratification by nonexistent chambers. This article itself remains proposed and is not a self-authorizing adoption instrument.
