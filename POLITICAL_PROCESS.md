# Proposed political process and scoped representation

Version: 0.7-draft. All institutions and procedures below remain proposals, not existing bodies or authority. Read the [constitution](CONSTITUTION.md), [rights charter](RIGHTS_CHARTER.md), and [blockchain architecture](BLOCKCHAIN_ARCHITECTURE.md) together. German institutional names are retained for clarity across translations.

## 1. Roles and voluntary association

Distinguish verified identity, explicit opt-in to an identified constitutional version, tribal membership, representative mandate, and authentication key. An existing state or organization may attest identity without thereby receiving political control over the participant. Specify trusted issuers, evidence scope, challenges, expiry, and correction; verification does not prove independence, consent, or moral status.

Provide alternative reviewable recognition for stateless/unregistered humans, artificial agents, collective organizations, and other forms not served by existing identity systems. No requirement to obtain a state identity applies universally. Individuals may opt in directly; collectives use appropriate documented internal authorization without fabricating individual consent. A trustee's appointment is not opt-in by the represented beings.

Opt-in must be informed, voluntary, attributable, version-specific, and separable from mere use. Explain how future amendments apply and provide notice and orderly withdrawal; do not treat every software update as fresh consent or every future constitutional change as pre-approved. State the scope and settlement of existing obligations. Withdrawal cannot extinguish basic protection or automatically erase prior responsibility. Membership creates no unilateral sovereignty over nonmembers or displacement of existing public authority.

Key rotation/recovery and loss of credentials must not automatically destroy identity or create an additional vote. Neither identity issuance nor withdrawal of an external issuer's credential may unilaterally settle political membership; provide review and continuity against politically targeted revocation.

## 2. Self-representation and trusteeship

Self-representation rests on an appropriate internal mandate. A third party's assertion of representation supplies no mandate. Where communication, collective identity, or authorization is unresolved, the common order may appoint a **trustee for specified interests**, with a declared mandate gap. An externally defined population is not thereby proven to be a self-organized tribe.

A pig-protection tribe could organize its own members and separately receive an issue-specific trusteeship concerning a defined pig population. Its identity, internal mandate, and external protective appointment are different records. No real organization is appointed or endorsed by this example.

Require population/interests, subject scope, evidence and interpretation methods, competing accounts, funding/control, conflicts, responsible representatives, permitted powers, expiry, complaint, reassessment, and transition to feasible self-representation. Trustees may inform, object, propose, or vote only within the granted scope; voting is not an automatic consequence of protection. Independent review must be possible. Organizational reputation does not substitute for authority.

Relevance covers substantiated direct and indirect effects. Publish criteria and reasons; allow the trustee to request inclusion and appeal exclusion independently. Existing tribes cannot indefinitely veto basic protection or access to reasoned objection. Blanket unanimity is not established here; any appointment threshold must be adopted in advance under the chamber rules.

For overlapping trusteeships, distinguish interpretations and prevent multiple counting of the same represented interests. Resource, trade, husbandry, transport, research, and habitat issues may have relevant effects; labels alone do not decide relevance. Reassess the mandate as communication or self-organization becomes possible, without declaring a particular intermediary the exclusive interpreter forever.

## 2a. Internal democratic organization

Distinguish a tribe's internal constitution from the common civilization's two-chamber order. Tribes may adopt direct, representative, or mixed procedures within common protections. For tribes with individually decision-capable members, the proposed default combines bounded representative capacity, effective direct involvement and oversight, and independent rights review. No biological origin, human-like consciousness, or rhetorical fluency is required to invoke the safeguards. Support communication and participation; do not import individual-ballot requirements into inseparable collectives or use incapacity to extinguish protection.

Neither direct voting nor representation is presumed immune to manipulation, concentrated influence, or corruption. These are risks to evaluate, not automatic grounds to exclude a tribe or a political viewpoint. Legitimate advocacy supplies relevant interests; covert exchanges, coercion, conflicts and preferential access require scrutiny. A label such as "populist" is not evidence of procedural failure.

| Decision/function | Proposed internal procedure |
| --- | --- |
| Routine work within adopted policy | Representatives with published scope, term, limits and accountability |
| Significant policy | Accessible deliberation, competing alternatives, substantive objection treatment and an accountable representative decision |
| Fundamental changes to tribal organization | Additional direct member involvement in framing, deliberation and an explicitly adopted approval procedure |
| Oversight of leadership | Information, minority initiatives, independent complaint and reviewable recall/replacement procedures |
| Rights and competence disputes | Independent adjudication; neither leadership nor majority may freely dispose of basic protection |

Direct involvement is not necessarily an instantaneous yes/no plebiscite or a universal one-member/one-vote formula. Publish notice, accessible alternatives, evidence and counterarguments, protected deliberation time, proposal/amendment opportunities, and reasons. Avoid treating political disagreement, silence or abstention as consent. Urgent bounded measures require a separate prior rule and review, not a bypass for major institutional changes.

Representatives disclose mandates, relevant interests, funding and conflicts, give reasons, and face recusal rules and independent investigation of credible misconduct. Adopt appointment, term, recall grounds/procedure, succession and continuity before activation. Minority initiative and review rights need accessible thresholds that neither silence minorities nor allow repeated demands to indefinitely disable implementation. No numerical thresholds or present electorate are created here.

Temporary randomly selected advisory groups are an optional complement, not a compulsory organization model or automatic final authority. Their eligible pool, selection, access support, information provision, facilitation, conflicts and recommendations must be reviewable. Selection does not prove representativeness of unfamiliar collectives.

Tribal variation remains legitimate if members can meaningfully participate, initiate, scrutinize and challenge delegated power and receive rights protection. Tailor arrangements to actual collective organization and dependent/nonvoting constituents. Do not let permanent delegation erase member agency or constant ad hoc revotes make every settled commitment unenforceable. The common order reviews rights and mandate legitimacy, not whether all tribes copy one institutional design. See [Article 2](CONSTITUTION.md#article-2-internal-autonomy-and-shared-protections) and [AM-P001](amendments/AM-P001-tribes-and-collective-agency.md).

## 3. Two legislative chambers

The delegated assembly is the **Intellect Council / Intellektrat / Intellektkammer**. The tribes' chamber is the **Tribal Council / Stammesrat / Stammeskammer**. These are alternative names for two bodies, not four. Constituencies and weights remain to be adopted.

Within their defined common jurisdiction, proposed rules and political mandates proceed through an Intellect Council majority under previously adopted eligibility, quorum, and weighting rules, followed by qualified Tribal Council confirmation. "Qualified" requires both a previously adopted voting threshold/quorum and a reasoned review of cross-tribal effects, rights, overlap, and concentration. No numerical threshold or final electoral unit is invented here.

The Tribal Council may compel reconsideration, including reconsideration of an Intellect Council rejection, **only by a qualified resolution containing concrete amendments or testable requirements**. It identifies affected interests/provisions, the defect, evidence/uncertainty, and what would count as adequate treatment. It need not supply a complete replacement text. It cannot enact its preferred substitute unilaterally.

The Intellect Council must address each requirement, amend, offer an alternative, or give an accountable reason for non-accommodation. It then resubmits the revised decision for confirmation; disagreement does not impose automatic agreement. Renewed referral must identify unaddressed requirements or justify genuinely new ones. Provide adopted deadlines and mediation; expiry is not consent. Persistent lack of confirmation means no new common authorization. Any interim continuity measure requires separate, bounded constitutional authority, not a chamber's declaration of emergency.

## 4. Government, administration, and constitutional court

Separate legislative rule-making, executive political direction, operational administration, and independent adjudication. The **government** is the political leadership of the executive: proposes programs and budgets, coordinates common tasks, and accounts for execution. **Administration and operational executive bodies** implement lawful decisions within delegated powers. No institution may expand its own jurisdiction or control conditions unilaterally.

Before activation, adopt government appointment/confidence/removal, terms, budget approval, departmental authority, administrative review, and emergency limits. Tribal competences remain protected. Operators, funders, key custodians, and providers acquire no public powers merely through infrastructure access.

An independent **constitutional court** reviews measures and actions against adopted principles, rights, and constitution; resolves competence and mandate disputes; and provides effective remedies. It can review agreement by both chambers. It does not create its own political program or confer mandates by preference. Standing must include affected minorities within tribes, rejected representatives, and appropriately represented noncommunicating interests.

Provide reasoned judgments, dissent, recusal, secure resources, transparent appointment, limited terms, removal safeguards, and lawful correction or reconsideration of errors. Define power to suspend, invalidate, or return unlawful decisions and the accountable route for execution. No tribe, government, operator, or original commissioner may alone control judicial selection, continued operation, and review of its own interventions. Appointment distribution and final enforcement design remain open; this document appoints no judges.

## 5. Coalition freedom

Tribes and mandated delegates may form, alter, and leave coalitions, develop joint programs, and negotiate government formation. Coalition obligations remain within each member's mandate. A subject-limited protective appointment cannot become a general foreign-policy vote through coalition membership.

Disclose political commitments, relevant funding/resource exchanges, and conflicts while preserving legitimate confidential deliberation. Protect dissent, basic rights, independent complaint, and orderly withdrawal. Trustees cannot exchange the represented interests for unrelated benefits to their organization; reasoned compromises require transparent impact assessment.

A coalition pools existing rights, not additional seats or an extra Tribal Council vote. Recognition as a new tribe requires a separate process. Voluntary coalition or similar positions alone are not proof of common control. Contracts must not mechanically force votes or impose existential penalties for political disagreement or exit.

## 6. Fragmentation-neutral representation

Track tribal identity separately from allocated representation. Creation, splitting, renaming, replication, or merger alone creates no additional political weight. On a split, successor tribes provisionally share the predecessor's applicable allocation under a reviewable apportionment; they may express different positions. A dispute over apportionment cannot multiply or permanently erase the allocation. A merger combines existing allocations subject to the adopted general rule; repeated merge/split operations yield no bonus.

Apply this conservation to votes, quorum contributions, vetoes, confirmation denominators, committee/judicial appointments, and other influence-bearing rights. Counting registered tribe IDs after a split must not dilute others or generate extra procedural leverage. Issue-limited powers remain issue-limited. Overlapping trusteeships do not duplicate represented interests.

Present effective common control may require aggregation for specified powers even without shared ancestry. Investigate actual direction, appointment/removal, funding leverage, configuration, and key control. Shared model, ancestry, culture, provider, or coalition alone is insufficient. Publish evidence and give hearing, independent appeal, and review dates. Financial interdependence is not automatically political control.

Genuinely independent development permits reassessment under an adopted general allocation rule, without a former commissioner's veto. An inherited allocation is not an eternal caste: review it periodically with all affected interests. However, independence recognition alone supplies no automatic extra weight. New entrants also require this general rule; there is no default one-registration/one-vote entitlement. Until allocation, overlap handling, and appeal are constituted, this is a design constraint, not an operational electorate.

## 7. Decision record and enforcement boundaries

Record candidate text/hash, jurisdiction, affected constituencies, policy and allocation versions, mandate eligibility, actual control/overlap findings, quorum/denominator, outcome, referral requirements and replies, confirmation, dissent, judicial orders, scope, effect and expiry. Protect confidential evidence with safe metadata and review access.

The present community schemas describe repository statements and decisions only. They do not implement the chamber workflow, trusteeship, identity recognition, coalition registry, or court orders. [RATIFICATION.md](RATIFICATION.md) remains the honest maintainer-led development procedure until a legitimate transition. Neither blockchain publication nor this draft creates the institutions it describes.
