# Amendment proposals and normative history

Status: all current candidates are **proposed**. Nothing here has been ratified or activated. Proposal IDs are not numbers of adopted constitutional amendments.

The [manifesto](../MANIFEST.md) separates foundational principles, constitutional order, amendments, and implementation protocols. The [constitution](../CONSTITUTION.md) is also proposed. The model borrows traceable addition/repeal from amendment practice; it does not claim the US process is inherently easier to revise than the German one. ## Lifecycle and records

`proposed -> under_review -> ratified -> active -> superseded/repealed`; a draft may instead become `withdrawn` or `rejected`. This is a conceptual lifecycle, not an implemented authenticated state machine. Never edit a previous decision to hide its history. A correction, withdrawal, replacement, or repeal creates a traceable new record/decision.

Each candidate needs purpose, text, relation to principles, affected provisions, evidence/uncertainty, alternatives, objections, representation impacts, adoption route, and review conditions. Ratification requires a valid decision reference and exact text commit; activation also needs an effective date and applicable transition provisions. A non-null reference is not proof that its decision is legitimate. Absence of a decision means no adoption.

**Ordinary amendments** add/refine constitutional provisions within the principles. **Foundational revisions** modify the principles or the scope of basic protection and need a separately adopted protective process. Technical formats cannot smuggle in either type. No absolute eternity clause or final supermajority formula is declared in this draft.

## Precedence and consolidated reading

Read adopted principles with the adopted constitution and amendments. An amendment may replace/repeal an identified earlier provision only through the competent procedure; a lower-level protocol cannot do so. Unresolved conflict requires independent review, not a "latest file wins" rule. Keep a consolidated text plus links to source text, scope, adoption decision, dates, and superseded provisions.

There is currently no consolidated **effective** constitution because there is no ratified founding corpus. MANIFEST.md and CONSTITUTION.md provide a consolidated **proposed design**. Git history is a preservation tool, not a substitute for normative decision records.
