# AM-P007 — Voluntary membership, scoped mandates, coalitions and aggregation

Status: proposed ordinary-amendment candidate, draft 0.2; no ratification or effective date. Related principles: I–IV. Proposed scope: constitutional Articles 7, 9–10, 12. No supplied law wording is changed.

## Proposed provisions, alternatives, and review

The proposed text is constitutional Articles 9–10 read with POLITICAL_PROCESS.md sections 1–2 and 5–7. Separate identity attestations, opt-in, tribal membership, authorization, and keys; provide alternate recognition, privacy, recovery and orderly withdrawal. No identity issuer obtains political control through attestation.

Third-party trustees need explicit issue-relevant mandates, methods, conflicts, powers, expiry and independent appeal. Shared appointment cannot fabricate represented consent or self-organized tribal identity. Relevance decisions are contestable. Coalitions preserve mandate limits and create no extra weight. Splits, mergers, replication and overlapping trusteeships conserve allocated influence, including quorum, veto and appointment leverage. Current control is separate from ancestry, with independent reassessment under a general allocation rule.

Review gatekeeping, duplicate representation, indirect affectedness, captured trustees, coerced coalition voting, fake splits, identity-provider dependence, lost keys, and exclusion of unfamiliar forms. Alternatives include blanket mandates or one-registration/one-vote; examine their capture risks. Actual issuers, apportionment methods, eligibility, thresholds, recourse and jurisdiction remain unresolved. No blockchain deployment, public registry, or mandate is authorized.

## Adoption and authority

Read with the proposed rights charter and AM-P006; this candidate does not itself adopt that foundational revision. Classification remains challengeable: any effect changing principles or protected scope requires the separately adopted foundational-revision procedure. Final mandates, weights, appointments and institutions require prior legitimate rules. Repository publication and structural validation are not ratification. Preserve exact text/commit, objections, accountable decision, scope, transition and review date before any future activation.

## Transitional jurisdiction clarification

Include proposed [Article 12](../CONSTITUTION.md#article-12-universal-protection-limited-jurisdiction-and-public-enforcement) and [process section 8](../POLITICAL_PROCESS.md#8-transitional-jurisdiction-and-public-enforcement). Universal moral protection is distinct from membership and actual public jurisdiction. Preserve symmetric accession safeguards and reassessment of artificial autonomy; operator constraints do not prove consent. Use competent existing legislative, judicial and executive mechanisms for recognized claims while internal bodies exercise only actually entrusted authority. Protection gaps call for lawful support and legitimate legal development, not private coercion. Further public competence requires separate legitimate external authorization. Review conflicts of law, access to remedies, internal-court limits, unsupported legal-status claims and asymmetrical opt-in. No ordinary amendment can itself transfer state powers or change protected scope.
