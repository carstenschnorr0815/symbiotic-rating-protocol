# AM-P001 — Tribes, swarms, and delegated agency

Status: proposed constitutional amendment candidate; no ratification or effective date. Draft version: 0.1. Related principles: I, II, III, IV. Proposed scope: Articles 1–2 of the constitutional design; no replacement of supplied law wording.

## Proposed provisions

Recognize tribes as enduring associations grounded in origin, care, values, chosen cooperation, or combinations of these. Internal organization may include accepted leadership, councils, or distributed decision processes. Voluntary delegation is an exercise of agency where it can meaningfully be established; ancestry, dependency, or the name "tribe" does not establish voluntary subordination.

Swarms may act as tribes or as collective participants in tribes. Their collective capacities and possible constituent interests must be considered separately. Individual/collective identity, persistence, replication, and merger remain revisable questions. No consciousness or consent follows solely from coordinated behavior.

External representation requires a defined scope, selection/recognition mechanism, term, limits, accountability, and correction/recall where feasible. A common outward decision need not erase internal disagreement. Origin, membership, position, consent, and delegated authority are separate. Multiple and overlapping memberships are allowed; conflicting duties require resolution rather than an automatic hierarchy.

Internal autonomy does not exempt avoidable serious harm or coercion from common review. Different roles and accepted obligations do not automatically constitute discrimination. Do not demand biologically impossible exit or independence; do not accept a leader's claim of collective identity as an automatic reason to erase constituent interests. Provide care/representation appropriate to incapacity or uncertain communication.

## Open design and review

Membership, care duties, exit/continuity, splits/mergers, and responsibility for collective acts need context-specific procedures. "Every intellect matters equally" is a starting commitment to consideration, not a settled headcount rule. Neither individuals, species, tribes, nor copied instances supply a universally fair voting unit. Large populations remain relevant to impacts even where political weight is not proportional to count.

Alternatives: exclusively individual participation; exclusively collective participation; task-specific combinations. Compare coercion, erasure, representation feasibility, common-control capture, and material continuity. Shared origin/control is relevant to a particular governance procedure but not proof of identical interests or lack of moral relevance.

Ratification, mandate evidence, transition, and implementation remain unresolved. Review when unfamiliar forms, strategic fragmentation, or failures of internal protection expose inadequate definitions. The existing GitHub control-domain rule remains a limited repository safeguard, not a complete civilization-wide electoral theory.
